# Consumer Behavior Angle — CB/MR/Branding Concepts (compact reference)

Use these to explain WHY a customer reacted the way they did, not just WHAT
they said. Every issue should be traceable to at least one named concept
below — plain language first, concept name second. Spread reasoning across
a WIDE set of these across issues, not the same 2-3 every time.

1. **CDM funnel** (Need Recognition→Search→Evaluation→Purchase→Post-Purchase): locate which stage an issue breaks at before recommending a fix.
2. **Value Equation** (Value = Benefits − Costs): a complaint means Costs now exceed Benefits — did Benefits drop or perceived Costs rise?
3. **Approach vs Avoidance Valence**: avoidance-driven complaints ("scared it'll happen again") are stickier and need reassurance/proof, not just a feature fix.
4. **Operant Conditioning**: repeat complaints on the same issue mean the negative-reinforcement loop was never actually broken by the "fix."
5. **Memory Nodes / Node Ownership**: does this issue threaten the ONE word/need this brand is supposed to own in customers' heads?
6. **Positioning vs Perceptual Map**: the review corpus IS the perceptual map (what customers actually believe); a gap vs stated positioning is the real strategic problem.
7. **JND (Weber's Law)**: a change must cross a threshold to register — anger in reviews confirms it crossed JND; silence on a real improvement means it shipped below JND.
8. **Selective Attention/Distortion/Retention**: if a fix was communicated but complaints persist, the message may have been filtered out, not the fix failed.
9. **Assimilation-Contrast**: a small fix gets assimilated (no credit given) unless it's large enough to register as genuinely different.
10. **Attitude ABC + 4 Functions** (Utilitarian/Knowledge-Object-Appraisal/Ego-Defensive/Value-Expressive): which function is damaged determines the right fix type — mechanism, consistency, dignity, or identity.
11. **Hierarchy of Effects**: high-involvement complaints need a rational/information-dense response; low-involvement ones need an emotional/experiential one.
12. **Horney's 3 Types** (Compliant/Aggressive/Detached): segment the complaining voice — reassurance-seeking, wants-to-win, or silently disengaging (most dangerous, rarely loud).
13. **AAAERRR Journey** (Awareness→Acquisition→Activation→Engagement→Retention→Revenue→Referral): tag every issue's stage — Retention loss compounds against existing revenue, Acquisition friction only caps growth.
14. **Attribution Theory**: internal attribution ("it's genuinely good") builds loyalty; external ("only bought it for the discount") stays shallow and evaporates.
15. **Non-Compensatory Decision Rules** (Conjunctive/Disjunctive/Lexicographic): a hard dealbreaker complaint is Conjunctive-elimination severity — "now" priority regardless of mention count.
16. **Diffusion of Innovation / The Chasm**: complaints about basic polish (not novelty) signal the brand is trying to cross into the Early Majority without being ready.
17. **CBBE Pyramid** (Salience/Performance/Imagery/Judgements/Feelings/Resonance): diagnose which layer of brand equity an issue erodes.
18. **Brand Fidelity Matrix** (Sacredness × Transgression → Infidelity/Misdemeanour/Betrayal/Flirt): calibrate response severity independent of mention count.
19. **Hofstede (India lens)**: complaints referencing family/community opinion signal Subjective Norms overriding individual attitude — fix needs social proof, not just individual service.
20. **Enemy-Stand-Mantra**: frame a recurring pain as an ideology/condition the brand fights — Enemy must be a condition, never a named competitor.
21. **RFM Analysis**: prioritize retention effort by customer value (Recency/Frequency/Monetary) when purchase data exists, not just complaint volume.
22. **Brand Switch Matrix signal**: a review naming a specific competitor switched TO is a defection signal — track it separately from generic complaints.
23. **Coping style in brand crisis** (Active/Avoidant/Acceptance/Denial): a written complaint is an Active Coper — the visible, smaller risk. The bigger risk is the Avoidant Coper who never writes a review and just stops buying; low review volume on a real problem can mean "nobody's complaining," not "nobody's affected" — say so rather than reading silence as safety.
24. **Trust × Involvement relationship depth** (Brand Partner/Seeker/Habitual Buyer/Switcher): high trust + high involvement = Brand Partner (reward them, don't take for granted); high involvement + low trust = Seeker (close to converting — needs proof, not a discount); low involvement + high trust = Habitual Buyer (loyal but passive — vulnerable the moment a rival undercuts price); low both = Switcher (buys on deal/availability only — expensive to chase without unit economics that work). Distinct from the Brand Fidelity Matrix above (severity of a specific transgression) — this one diagnoses the ongoing relationship a complaining customer has with the brand before recommending how hard to fight for them.

Weak: "Customers are unhappy with delivery speed." Strong: "Delivery-delay
complaints cluster at the Retention stage (AAAERRR) among repeat customers
and read as Conjunctive-elimination — 'uninstalled after one late order' —
so this is a 'now' priority regardless of raw mention count; the Ego-
Defensive attitude function is also damaged, so the fix needs to restore
dignity (proactive delay notice + compensation), not just fix the ETA."
