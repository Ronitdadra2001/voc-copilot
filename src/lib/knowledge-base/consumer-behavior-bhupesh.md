# Consumer Behavior Angle — Bhupesh's CB/MR/Branding Concepts

Use these to explain WHY a customer reacted the way they did, not just WHAT they
said. A review is the visible symptom; these concepts diagnose the underlying
psychological mechanism. Every issue you analyze should be traceable to at
least one named concept below — plain language first, concept name second,
never a framework name dropped with no plain-English anchor. Across a full
report, deliberately draw on a WIDE spread of these (not the same 2-3 every
time) — that breadth is the point of this file.

## 1. The 5-Stage Consumer Decision-Making (CDM) funnel
Need Recognition → Search for Alternatives → Evaluation of Alternatives →
Purchase/Consumption → Post-Purchase Evaluation. Locate which stage a
complaint actually breaks at before recommending a fix — a Post-Purchase
complaint needs a service fix, a Search-stage complaint needs a discovery/SEO
fix. Never treat all complaints as the same stage.

## 2. Motivation & the Value Equation
Consumers move toward whichever option has the highest Value = Benefits −
Costs. A complaint is really a claim that Costs now exceed Benefits for that
customer. Ask: did Benefits drop (quality issue) or did perceived Costs rise
(price, effort, wait time, trust)?

## 3. Approach vs Avoidance Valence
Approach valence = moving toward a positive outcome; Avoidance valence =
moving away from a negative one. Avoidance-driven complaints ("I'm scared
this will happen again") are naturally higher-involvement and stickier than
approach-driven ones ("I wish it did X better") — they need reassurance/proof,
not just a feature fix.

## 4. Operant Conditioning / Reinforcement
Repeat complaints about the same issue mean the negative reinforcement loop
was never actually broken — the customer keeps re-experiencing the pain after
each supposed "fix." A fix that isn't confirmed to remove the pain (not just
ship code) doesn't count as resolved in the customer's conditioning history.

## 5. Memory Nodes & Node Ownership
Brands own a "need node" in customers' heads (the word/need they're
associated with) through repeated classical/operant/vicarious conditioning.
Ask: does this recurring complaint threaten the ONE word this brand is
supposed to own? A reliability brand getting reliability complaints is a
direct node-level threat, not just a feature bug.

## 6. Positioning Map vs Perceptual Map
Positioning = what the brand WANTS customers to believe (aspiration).
Perceptual = what customers actually believe, visible directly in review
text. The review corpus IS the perceptual map — when it contradicts the
brand's stated positioning, that gap is the real strategic problem, not any
single complaint.

## 7. Just Noticeable Difference (JND) / Weber's Law
A change (price hike, portion cut, quality shift) must cross a threshold
relative to the base value to be consciously noticed. If reviews show anger
at a change, it crossed JND — that's a bigger signal than the raw % suggests.
If a genuine improvement isn't mentioned in reviews at all, it likely
shipped below JND and customers literally didn't register it — don't market
it as if they will.

## 8. Selective Attention & the 3 Communication Barriers
Customers filter out unwanted stimuli via Selective Exposure (skip it),
Selective Distortion (twist it to fit existing belief), and Selective
Retention (only remember what confirms prior belief). If a fix was
communicated but complaints persist, check whether the message was actually
filtered out via one of these three, not whether the fix itself failed.

## 9. Assimilation-Contrast Theory
A small change gets assimilated (absorbed into the existing impression,
customer gives no credit) unless it's large enough to trigger Contrast
(perceived as genuinely different). An incremental fix to a badly-damaged
issue risks being assimilated away and wasting the investment — sometimes
the fix needs to be dramatic enough to register, not just directionally
correct.

## 10. Attitude ABC Model + the 4 Functions of Attitude
Attitude = Affect (feel) + Behavior (do) + Cognition (know). Every negative
attitude serves one of four functions, and the function determines the right
fix:
- **Utilitarian** — broken direct reward/pain (bug, late delivery) → fix the
  mechanism.
- **Knowledge/Object-Appraisal** — attitude as a decision shortcut ("X brand
  = always reliable") broken by inconsistency → fix requires CONSISTENCY
  over one-off great experiences.
- **Ego-Defensive** — the complaint protects the customer's self-image (felt
  embarrassed, judged, unsafe) → fix must restore dignity, not just the
  mechanism.
- **Value-Expressive** — the brand stopped saying what the customer wants it
  to say about them → fix is identity/brand-level, not operational.

## 11. Hierarchy of Effects (involvement-dependent)
High-involvement purchases follow Know→Feel→Do; low-involvement follow
Do→Feel→Know; experiential/impulse follow Feel→Do→Know. A high-involvement
complaint (e.g. enterprise plan, big-ticket item) needs a rational,
information-dense fix response; a low-involvement complaint (a snack, a
quick-commerce order) needs an emotional/experiential fix, not a spec sheet.

## 12. Horney's 3 Personality Types (segmenting the complaining voice)
- **Compliant** (moves toward people, fears rejection) — writes reviews
  seeking reassurance/community; respond with warmth and belonging.
- **Aggressive** (moves against people, fears weakness) — writes reviews to
  win/be right/demand status; respond with decisive action and visible
  authority.
- **Detached** (moves away from people, fears dependence) — quietly
  disengages, rarely complains loudly, just leaves; the silent churners you
  won't see in loud reviews — the most dangerous segment to miss.

## 13. The AAAERRR Consumer Journey
Awareness → Acquisition → Activation → Engagement → Retention → Revenue →
Referral. Tag every issue by which stage it breaks — an Activation-stage
issue (bad first-use) is not equally urgent to a Retention-stage issue
(existing paying customers leaving); Retention loss compounds against
existing revenue, Activation friction only caps future growth.

## 14. Attribution Theory (internal vs external)
If a customer attributes a good outcome to something INTERNAL to the brand
("it's genuinely good"), loyalty forms. If EXTERNAL ("only bought it because
of the discount"), the relationship stays shallow and evaporates once the
incentive ends. Heavy discount-driven acquisition that later complains about
value is an attribution problem, not a product problem — fix pricing
communication, not the discount itself.

## 15. Non-Compensatory Decision Rules
- **Conjunctive** — a minimum cutoff on every attribute; failing ANY one
  eliminates the brand outright (a single dealbreaker bug = full rejection
  regardless of other strengths).
- **Disjunctive** — exceeding the ideal on just ONE attribute is enough to
  win, regardless of weaknesses elsewhere.
- **Lexicographic** — ranked by importance, decided on the single
  most-important attribute.
An issue that reads as a hard dealbreaker ("uninstalled immediately because
of X") is Conjunctive-elimination severity — treat as "now" priority
regardless of mention count, because it silently disqualifies the brand for
everyone who hits it, not just the reviewers who mentioned it.

## 16. Diffusion of Innovation & the Chasm
Innovators/Early Adopters tolerate rough edges and rely on their own
judgment; the Early Majority waits for social proof and needs polish/trust
signals before adopting. If complaints increasingly reference "expected
better for a brand this size" or basic reliability (not novelty), that's a
signal the brand is trying to cross the chasm into the Early Majority and
the product isn't yet polished enough for that audience.

## 17. CBBE Pyramid (Salience → Performance/Imagery → Judgements/Feelings → Resonance)
Every issue erodes one specific layer: Salience (do they even recall us
positively?), Performance (does it work as claimed?), Imagery (what does
using it say about them?), Judgements (quality/credibility/superiority
verdicts), Feelings (warmth/security/social approval), Resonance (loyalty,
community, advocacy). Diagnose the WEAKEST layer the review evidence points
to — that's the layer investment should go to first, not necessarily the
loudest complaint category.

## 18. Brand Fidelity Matrix (Perceived Sacredness × Perceived Transgression)
- High sacredness + high transgression = **Infidelity** (severe backlash;
  the brand broke its most sacred promise).
- High sacredness + low transgression = **Misdemeanour** (forgiven, minor
  slip from a trusted brand).
- Low sacredness + high transgression = **Betrayal** ("I trusted you with
  something important and you failed me" — even from a brand with no prior
  premium image, this cuts deep, e.g. safety/health failures).
- Low sacredness + low transgression = **Flirt** (a minor, easily-forgotten
  complaint).
Use this to calibrate response severity — a Betrayal-tier issue (trust/safety)
needs a different urgency and tone than a Flirt-tier one, independent of
mention count.

## 19. Hofstede's Cultural Dimensions (India-market lens)
India skews high Power Distance, low Individualism (collectivist/family-
first), high Long-Term Orientation, low Indulgence. Complaints that reference
family/community opinion ("my family won't let me," "everyone I know
switched to X") signal Subjective Norms overriding individual attitude —
common in India — meaning the fix must address social proof/reputation, not
just the individual customer's experience.

## 20. Enemy-Stand-Mantra (ESM)
Every strong brand response to a recurring pain point can be framed as
fighting an ideology/condition (the Enemy), taking a Stand, and distilling it
into a Mantra — the Enemy must be a condition/behavior, never a named
competitor. Use this ONLY when a real, recurring customer pain clearly
justifies a public stance; do not force one onto a minor operational issue.

## 21. RFM Analysis (Recency, Frequency, Monetary)
When financial/purchase context is available, prioritize retention effort by
customer value, not just complaint volume: a high-value, high-frequency
customer (555-type) voicing a complaint is a different priority than a
one-time low-spend complainer (111-type) — protect the former first.

## 22. Brand Switch Matrix signal
When reviews explicitly name a competitor they switched TO, that is a
defection signal worth tracking separately from generic complaints — it
tells you exactly who is winning your unhappy customers and lets you target
a conquest strategy back.

## Output discipline
Weak: "Customers are unhappy with delivery speed."
Strong: "Delivery-delay complaints cluster at the Retention stage of the
AAAERRR journey (repeat customers, not first-timers) and read as Conjunctive-
elimination — 'uninstalled after one late order' is a hard dealbreaker, not a
scored trade-off — so this is a 'now' priority regardless of raw mention
count; the Ego-Defensive attitude function is also damaged (customers felt
foolish for trusting the ETA), so the fix must restore dignity (proactive
delay notice + compensation) on top of the operational fix."
